# Catalyst OS Risk Register v0.2

This document outlines the risks associated with the development of Catalyst OS, their potential impact, and mitigation strategies.

| ID | Category | Description | Likelihood | Impact | Risk Score | Mitigation Strategy | Contingency Plan | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | Technical | Kernel stability issues leading to frequent panics. | Medium | Critical | High | Rigorous unit testing, continuous fuzzing, strict Rust safety enforcement. | Rollback to stable kernel snapshot; dedicate team to triage. | Kernel Architect |
| R02 | Technical | Hardware compatibility limited to specific x86-64 devices. | High | High | High | Define strict hardware baseline early; prioritize standard UEFI/ACPI compliance. | Limit initial release scope to certified hardware list. | Driver Lead |
| R03 | Technical | Failure to meet Performance Contract targets. | Medium | High | High | Continuous performance tracking in CI; aggressive profiling (flamegraphs). | Re-evaluate architecture; optimize hot paths (e.g., IPC) in NASM. | Performance Engineer |
| R04 | Architectural | Hybrid microkernel IPC overhead proves too high. | Medium | High | High | Implement fast-path IPC (e.g., synchronous endpoints, lockless queues). | Migrate critical services back into the kernel space. | OS Architect |
| R05 | Architectural | Driver model complexity hinders third-party development. | Medium | Medium | Medium | Create comprehensive documentation, DDK (Driver Development Kit), and templates. | Internalize critical driver development. | OS Architect |
| R06 | Architectural | Improper synchronization leads to deadlocks in core kernel. | Low | Critical | Medium | Use static analysis tools; minimize lock contention; design lock-free structures. | Kernel panic dumps; immediate hotfix deployment. | Kernel Architect |
| R07 | Compatibility | Win32 compatibility layer (if planned) is incomplete. | High | High | High | Prioritize high-value APIs; leverage Wine ecosystem knowledge. | Focus on POSIX/native apps; pivot target market. | Userspace Lead |
| R08 | Compatibility | Modern anti-cheat systems refuse to run on Catalyst OS. | High | Critical | High | Engage with anti-cheat vendors early; implement robust secure boot and attestation. | Abandon gaming market; focus on workstation/server use cases. | Security Lead |
| R09 | Resource | Developer bandwidth exhausted by scope of OS development. | High | High | High | Strict scope management; prioritize MVP features; agile development cycles. | Descope non-essential features; extend timeline. | Project Manager |
| R10 | Resource | Knowledge gaps in specific hardware specifications (e.g., PCIe, USB4). | Medium | Medium | Medium | Budget for training/consultants; acquire detailed specification documents. | Use fallback legacy modes (e.g., USB 2.0) initially. | Driver Lead |
| R11 | Resource | Build times become a bottleneck for developer productivity. | High | Medium | Medium | Modularize compilation; use sccache/mold; invest in powerful build servers. | Reduce frequency of full builds; rely on host unit testing. | DevOps Lead |
| R12 | Legal | Unintentional GPL contamination from referencing Linux source code. | Medium | Critical | High | Clean-room design policy; strictly prohibit copying code from GPL projects. | Code audit; rewrite contaminated modules. | Legal / OS Architect |
| R13 | Legal | Patent infringement on OS concepts or file systems. | Low | High | Medium | Prior art research; use unencumbered or licensed technologies (e.g., standard FAT). | Redesign infringing components; seek licensing agreements. | Legal |
| R14 | Legal | Reverse engineering laws violated during compatibility layer development. | Low | High | Medium | Ensure clean-room reverse engineering practices; document all processes. | Halt development of affected components. | Legal |
| R15 | Scope | Scope creep due to new feature requests. | High | Medium | High | Strict change control board (CCB); adhere to MVP definition. | Defer requests to post-1.0 milestones. | Project Manager |
| R16 | Scope | Feature bloat degrading overall system performance. | Medium | High | Medium | Enforce Performance Contract; require profiling for new features. | Revert features; enforce strict modularity. | OS Architect |
| R17 | Data Safety | Source code leak of proprietary Catalyst OS. | Low | Critical | Medium | Strict access controls, NDA enforcement, secure VCS hosting. | Incident response plan; public relations strategy. | Security Lead |
| R18 | Data Safety | Loss of development data due to infrastructure failure. | Low | High | Medium | Implement 3-2-1 backup strategy; automated off-site backups. | Restore from latest available backup. | DevOps Lead |
| R19 | Long-term | Inability to build a developer ecosystem or community. | High | High | High | Provide excellent documentation, SDKs, and developer evangelism. | Focus on enterprise/embedded B2B sales instead of general consumer. | Developer Relations |
| R20 | Long-term | Maintainability issues due to complex codebases. | Medium | Medium | Medium | Enforce strict coding standards (clippy); mandate thorough documentation. | Dedicated refactoring sprints. | OS Architect |
| R21 | Technical | UEFI bootloader fails on certain obscure motherboards. | Medium | High | Medium | Test on a wide variety of physical hardware early; fallback legacy boot (if supported). | Blacklist problematic motherboards in documentation. | Bootloader Lead |
| R22 | Security | Sandbox escape vulnerability in userspace services. | Medium | Critical | High | Formal verification of capability system; rigorous security audits. | Immediate patch deployment; temporary disabling of affected service. | Security Lead |
| R23 | Technical | Memory leaks in long-running user space server (e.g., GUI server). | High | High | High | Automated long-duration stress testing; integrate leak sanitizers. | Automatic restart mechanisms for crashed/leaking services. | Userspace Lead |
| R24 | Architectural | VFS design fails to scale to high concurrent IO. | Medium | High | Medium | Benchmark VFS early; study scalable designs (e.g., Linux dcache). | Redesign VFS layer; use per-CPU data structures. | Filesystem Lead |
| R25 | Compatibility | Failure to support crucial modern hardware (e.g., NVMe). | Medium | High | High | Prioritize NVMe driver development; allocate dedicated resources. | Fallback to SATA/AHCI; limit target hardware. | Driver Lead |
| R26 | Resource | Lack of funding to complete development. | Low | Critical | Low | Maintain clear milestone deliverables to satisfy stakeholders/investors. | Open-source core components to attract community help. | Project Manager |
| RISK-C01 | Compatibility | Windows compatibility complexity — Win32 API surface is enormous | HIGH | CRITICAL | High | Scope down supported APIs to common game/app requirements; leverage wine subsets. | Support only critical enterprise apps, abandon full game compatibility. | Compatibility Lead |
| RISK-C02 | Technical | Modern GPU driver complexity — each vendor is different | HIGH | CRITICAL | High | Focus on a single vendor first (e.g. AMD) with open documentation. | Rely on basic framebuffer until resources permit full driver team. | Graphics Lead |
| RISK-C03 | Compatibility | Anti-cheat incompatibility — kernel-level anti-cheat may refuse non-Windows | HIGH | HIGH | High | Proactive engagement with anti-cheat providers, secure attestation guarantees. | Market Catalyst for single-player gaming and workstation use only. | Security Architect |
| RISK-C04 | Technical | Hardware fragmentation — vast diversity of PC hardware | HIGH | CRITICAL | High | Maintain strict hardware compatibility lists; fallback generic drivers. | Ship as pre-installed OS on certified hardware appliances. | Hardware PM |
| RISK-C05 | Architectural | Kernel architecture lock-in — choosing wrong arch early is expensive to reverse | MEDIUM | CRITICAL | High | Maintain abstraction layers; decouple security and IPC models from kernel. | Plan major refactoring milestones if performance hits critical blockers. | Core Architect |
| RISK-C06 | Scope | Scope explosion — feature creep across 15 milestones | HIGH | CRITICAL | High | Ruthless prioritization; strictly enforce MVP requirements per milestone. | Cut major subsystems (e.g. custom GUI) and adopt open-source alternatives. | Product Owner |
| RISK-C07 | Technical | AI subsystem becoming resource-heavy — contradicting efficiency goals | MEDIUM | HIGH | High | Run AI tasks asynchronously; use quantized models; offload to NPU. | Make AI features opt-in or strictly cloud-based. | AI Lead |
| RISK-C08 | Architectural | Compatibility requirements contaminating Catalyst core — kernel becomes Windows emulator | HIGH | CRITICAL | High | Keep compatibility strictly in userspace sandboxed runtimes. | Abandon compatibility; focus solely on native Catalyst applications. | Architecture Board |
